Prologue

The ground gave way.

Cain wakes alone in the dark. Somewhere above, the fight is lost. The floor gave way, and the Aegis, the rune that has caught every blow ever meant for him, no longer holds a spark.

Duncan, Lucious and Wynflaed are up there, if they're still alive. Down here, the bloodroot keep hitting until something stops moving.

How it plays

Explore. Fight. Listen.

Exploration

One cave, two realms. What blocks the way on this side may not exist on the other. Shift across, climb, push, and find a way out.

Combat

Turn-based fights on a grid. Every move costs action points, where you stand matters, and the party's combos chain off each other. Swapping Cain's rune mid-fight costs his whole turn, and sometimes it is worth it.

Story

Told in small things: the bread Duncan won't eat, the straps he never says are spare, the hands Lucious counts. Cain keeps a journal, and he writes about the other three because they are the part he understands least.

Runes are how the knights grow. Victor, the Mage of Clifffall, carved them into their armour. Each rune has its own road of thirty levels, it grows with use, and every fight won teaches the one Cain is wearing.

Entry IThe realm

Older than ours

Cain can shift between two realms. The other one looks ancient. As if a mirror showed the world as it was long ago.

It is not the past. Nothing he does over there changes anything here. The only thing that crosses is what gets pulled through.

The realm sits between our realm and the plane mages use to expand their magical reach.

Entry IIWhat the demo holds

In the demo

The free demo picks up partway through Knightfall, just as everything goes wrong.

How runes work

  • Victor, the Mage of Clifffall, carved the runes worked into the knights' armour. Each knight wears one.
  • Cain finds more than one, and only one at a time fits his armour. Which he wears is the choice that matters.
  • A rune grows as it is used, and every fight won teaches the one he is wearing.

Entry IIIField notes

What lives down here

Bloodroot Thrall

common, the lower caves

threat
Low on its own
weakness
Patience. It does not pace itself.

Comes at whoever is nearest. Keeps hitting until something stops moving. Easier to fight than the wolves. Worse to look at. It mimics the movement of its prey.

Wisps

The other realm

threat
They seem friendly?
weakness
Unknown

They float around and give off a calming chime. They seem to appear and disappear at will. I haven't noticed them doing anything specific, mostly just wandering around.

Silvermane Fox

On higher ground

threat
None it has shown
weakness
None I would want to use

White along the shoulder and down the back, and it throws light the way water does, which nothing with fur should. I once caught one with Wyn for Victor.

Blood Monstrosity

at the end of the cave

threat
Highest I have seen
weakness
None I have found

Too large for the passage it occupies, which raises the question of how it came to be in it. I do not like either answer.
